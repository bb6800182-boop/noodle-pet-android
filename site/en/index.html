Lamian for Android

After too long on your phone, it walks in from the edge of the screen, gets you moving and drinking water, shows you the exercise and times it.

Why I made it

The original is Pete Cybriwsky (Instagram, opens in a new tab) 's Noodle (opens in a new tab): a little noodle that reminds you to stretch and demonstrates the exercise itself.

At the computer, Lamian for Windows already keeps an eye on me. But away from the desk I pick up my phone and half an hour is gone. Lamian for Android brings the noodle to the phone: it stays out of sight until you have been scrolling too long, then walks in from the edge and leads you through a stretch.

What it does

Everything is on the app's Settings tab. All numbers can be changed and are saved automatically.

Screen-time reminders

Only time with the screen on and unlocked counts. After 30 minutes in a row (adjustable) the noodle walks in from the edge; 5 minutes or more with the screen off counts as a break. If you ignore it, it leaves by itself and you can catch up within 30 minutes.

It shows you how

Eight stretches: the noodle demonstrates while the bubble explains, and Start begins the countdown. Three more for wrists, fingers and eyes can be turned on. Exercises can be switched on and off, retimed and reordered.

Water reminders

Every 60 minutes from 8:00 to 22:00 by default. The noodle drinks from a glass; tap “Drank it” to log one. It goes by the clock, whether or not you are on the phone.

Custom reminders

Every day, weekdays, chosen days or once. At the set time the noodle says your line, and can also lead an exercise or a water break. With the screen off it sends a notification, or waits until you unlock.

Out of your way

Hidden until a reminder is due, or peeking from the screen edge where a tap brings it out to say hi. It stays away during calls, and you can set quiet hours or pause for an hour.

Light on battery, offline

No constant background checks: it wakes only when the screen turns on or off, on unlock, or when an alarm fires, and the noodle is removed completely when put away. No network permission; data stays on the phone.

Screens

Reminders appear as a speech bubble above the noodle, on top of the app you are using. The screenshots show the Chinese interface; the app is in English on English phones.

Pick your companion

Five characters, switchable any time in Settings. You can also choose the size, bottom right or bottom left, and whether it walks in or drops from the top.

Download and install

  1. Download lamian.apk on your phoneIf you are reading this on a computer, send the address to your phone and open it there. If the browser warns that this type of file can harm your device, choose to download anyway.
  2. Allow the installTap the downloaded file. The first time you install from a browser, Android asks you to turn on “Allow from this source” in Settings; come back and tap Install. If Google Play Protect shows a notice, that is because the app is not from the Play Store; you can choose to install anyway.
  3. Open Lamian and grant what the home tab asks for“Display over other apps” has to be turned on in system settings so the noodle can appear over other apps; for Notifications just tap Allow. On some brands, also set “Battery usage” to “Unrestricted” (in the app under Settings → System) so the system does not close it.
  4. Try itOn the Today tab tap “Stretch now” and the noodle walks out to lead you through a stretch.
  5. Make it yoursIn Settings, change the reminder interval, water hours, character and size, and whether it hides or peeks when idle. Add custom reminders on the Reminders tab.

Questions

What do I need?

A phone with Android 13 or later. No sign-in and nothing else to install.

Does it drain the battery?

No. It does not keep checking in the background; it wakes only when the screen turns on or off, on unlock, when an alarm fires or the call state changes, and works out your screen time from those moments. The noodle is drawn only during a reminder and removed completely afterwards; only “Peek from side” keeps one small still image on screen.

There is always one Lamian notification: Android requires it for apps that keep running in the background. It shows today's counts and has buttons for “Had a glass”, “Stretch now” and “Pause 1 hour”.

Why these permissions?

“Display over other apps” lets the noodle and bubble appear over other apps; without it, reminders come as notifications. Notifications are used for the status notification and for reminders while the screen is off or you are on a call. Alarms keep water and custom reminders on time. Run at startup lets it start on boot; you can turn that off in Settings.

It does not need usage access, never reads other apps' content, and has no network permission.

Where is my data? Is it uploaded?

Settings, custom reminders and today's log are stored in the app's data on your phone. The app has no network permission, uploads nothing and has no analytics.

How do I pause it or turn it off?

For a break, tap “Pause 1 hour” on the bubble, the home tab or the notification.

To turn it off completely, switch off “Lamian on” at the top of Settings: no more reminders, the notification and the noodle are put away, and it does not start on boot. Today's log is kept, and everything comes back when you turn it on.

How do I update?

Come back to this page, download lamian.apk again and install it over the old version; settings and logs are kept. The current version is under Settings → About.

How do I remove it?

Long-press the Lamian icon and choose Uninstall, or remove it under the system's Settings → Apps. Settings and logs are deleted with it.

Is there a desktop version?

Yes, for Windows: Lamian desktop pet. They are separate apps and do not share settings or logs.

Is this the official Noodle?

No. Lamian for Android is an unofficial Android port, not affiliated with or endorsed by the author of Noodle. The characters, exercise demos and instructions come from Noodle (MIT License); both license texts are in the app under Settings → About → Licenses.

Are the stretches right for everyone?

The stretches are general activity suggestions, not medical advice. Stop if anything hurts or feels wrong; if you have an injury or condition, ask a doctor or physiotherapist first.
